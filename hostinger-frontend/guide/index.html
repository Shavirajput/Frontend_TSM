The Question

Your website looks fine. But is it working?

Most companies evaluate their website through questions like: Does it look professional? Is it mobile responsive? Is the branding good? Does Google like it?

Those questions matter. But they’re incomplete.

The more important question is: what is your website doing for the business?

  • 01Is it helping someone understand your offer?
  • 02Is it building enough trust to make them consider you?
  • 03Is it moving them toward action?
  • 04Is it capturing demand?
  • 05Is it helping your sales team?
  • 06Is it converting the attention you're already paying to acquire?

Because a website can be beautiful, fast, SEO-friendly, technically excellent… and still be a poor business asset.

“It looks great” and “it works” are two entirely different findings.

Definition

What is a revenue leak?

Definition

A revenue leak is a point of friction, confusion, mistrust or failure that prevents existing demand from moving further through the business.

It doesn’t necessarily mean “your website is losing exactly ₹X.” That would be dishonest without the underlying data.

Instead, we ask: where is the website creating measurable or plausible commercial exposure?

Table 04-A · Five common leaks
Weak positioning

Visitor doesn't understand the value.

↓Lower engagement.
Weak trust

Visitor understands the offer but doesn't believe the company.

↓Lower intent.
Conversion friction

Visitor wants the solution but struggles to take action.

↓Lost lead.
Poor lead qualification

The company generates leads but the wrong ones.

↓Sales inefficiency.
Poor performance

Visitor abandons before experiencing the website.

↓Lost opportunity.
We look for commercial exposure, not aesthetic opinions.

Why 144

The 144-parameter idea

Why 144? Because websites don’t fail in one dimension.

great traffic+terrible conversion
great conversion+terrible lead quality
great messaging+terrible trust
great technology+no demand

The 144 parameters let us look at the website from multiple angles rather than making a subjective judgment based on aesthetics.

What We Look For

The Conversion Leak

Imagine a visitor arrives on your website. They understand the offer. They’re interested. They’re ready to explore. Then…

Nothing is technically “broken.” But the journey is leaking intent. That’s a TSM Nexus finding.

What We Look For

The Trust Leak

Your website says: “We’re the best solution for your business.” But where’s the evidence?

The visitor isn’t necessarily thinking “this company is bad.” They’re thinking “I’m not sure.”

And uncertainty kills action.

What We Look For

The Clarity Leak

Your headline says: “Empowering businesses through innovative digital transformation.” It sounds impressive. But…

If the visitor has to solve those questions themselves, you’re creating cognitive friction.

What We Look For

The Performance Leak

A website can be visually impressive and technically expensive… while making someone wait. Slow pages. Heavy images. Unnecessary scripts. Poor mobile performance. Broken interactions.

The question isn’t

“Is your PageSpeed score 87?”

The question is

“Is technology getting in the way of the business objective?”
